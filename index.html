1.1 Physical Quantities & Measurement ▼
ScalarHas magnitude only. Examples: distance, speed, time, mass, energy, temperature.
VectorHas magnitude AND direction. Examples: force, weight, velocity, acceleration, momentum, electric field strength, gravitational field strength.

Measurement

  • Rulers and measuring cylinders → length and volume
  • Clocks / digital timers → time intervals
  • Average period: time 10 oscillations ÷ 10 reduces random error
Supplement — Resultant of two perpendicular vectors: Use Pythagoras: $R = \sqrt{A^2 + B^2}$. Direction: $\theta = \tan^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{B}{A}\right)$. Applies to forces or velocities only.
MCQ Trap: "A car travels at 60 km h⁻¹ north" is velocity. "60 km h⁻¹" alone is speed. Velocity MUST state direction.
1.2 Motion ★ HIGH YIELD ▼
Speed$v = \dfrac{s}{t}$ — distance per unit time. Scalar. Unit: m s⁻¹
VelocitySpeed in a given direction. Vector. Unit: m s⁻¹
Acceleration$a = \dfrac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$ — change in velocity per unit time. Unit: m s⁻²
Speed$v = \dfrac{s}{t}$
Average speed$\bar{v} = \dfrac{\text{total distance}}{\text{total time}}$
Acceleration$a = \dfrac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$
Free fall$g \approx 9.8 \text{ m s}^{-2}$

Left: Gradient of d–t graph = speed. Right: Gradient of s–t graph = acceleration; area = distance.

Reading d–t graphs

  • Horizontal line → at rest (gradient = 0)
  • Straight diagonal → constant speed (gradient = speed)
  • Curve steepening → accelerating
  • Curve flattening → decelerating

Reading s–t graphs

  • Horizontal → constant speed (zero acceleration)
  • Straight line sloping up → constant acceleration
  • Gradient = acceleration; area = distance
Supplement — Terminal Velocity: Object falling in fluid → weight > drag → accelerates. Speed ↑ → drag ↑ → eventually drag = weight → zero resultant force → terminal velocity (constant speed). Opening a parachute increases drag → decelerates to new lower terminal velocity.
MCQ Trap: Deceleration is negative acceleration. If decelerating, use a negative value in $F = ma$.
1.3 Mass & Weight ▼
MassQuantity of matter. Constant everywhere. Unit: kg.
WeightGravitational force: $W = mg$. Changes with $g$. Unit: N.
Gravitational field strength ($g$)Force per unit mass: $g = \dfrac{W}{m}$. On Earth $\approx 9.8$ N kg⁻¹. Numerically equals acceleration of free fall.
Weight$W = mg$
Key distinction: Mass (kg) is constant. Weight (N) changes. Same object: $W \approx 98$ N on Earth, $\approx 16$ N on Moon ($g_{\text{moon}} \approx 1.6$ N kg⁻¹).
1.4 Density ▼
Density ($\rho$)Mass per unit volume. Unit: kg m⁻³ or g cm⁻³.
Density$\rho = \dfrac{m}{V}$

Measuring Density

  • Regular solid: measure dimensions, compute $V$, weigh
  • Liquid: volume in measuring cylinder, mass by difference
  • Irregular solid: displacement — submerge in water, volume displaced = object volume

Object floats if $\rho_{\text{object}} < \rho_{\text{fluid}}$.

1.5 Forces ★ HIGH YIELD ▼
Newton's 1st LawObject remains at rest or constant velocity unless acted on by a resultant force.
Newton's 2nd Law ★$F = ma$ — resultant force and acceleration are in the same direction.
Newton's 2nd$F = ma$
Spring constant ★$k = \dfrac{F}{x}$
Moment$M = F \times d_\perp$

Left: Hooke's Law region (green) — extension proportional to load. Right: Principle of moments in equilibrium.

Friction

  • Solid friction: opposes motion between surfaces, produces heat
  • Drag: friction in liquids and gases

Moments

  • Principle of moments: $\sum$ clockwise moments $=$ $\sum$ anticlockwise moments (equilibrium)
  • Equilibrium: zero resultant force AND zero resultant moment

Centre of Gravity

  • Point where entire weight appears to act
  • Lower CoG and wider base → more stable
Supplement — Circular Motion: Force perpendicular to velocity provides centripetal acceleration. Force ↑ (mass, radius fixed) → speed ↑. Force ↑ (mass, speed fixed) → radius ↓.
MCQ Trap: Hooke's Law ($F \propto x$) ONLY holds below the limit of proportionality. Do not use $k = F/x$ beyond this point.
1.6 Momentum ★ (Supplement) ▼
Momentum$p = mv$. Vector. Unit: kg m s⁻¹.
Impulse$\text{impulse} = F\Delta t = \Delta(mv)$. Unit: N s.
Momentum$p = mv$
Impulse$F\Delta t = \Delta(mv)$
Resultant force$F = \dfrac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}$
Conservation of Momentum: In a closed system, total momentum before = total momentum after. Always assign + and − to directions.
Explosions from rest: Total initial $p = 0$, so $m_1v_1 + m_2v_2 = 0$ → objects move in opposite directions.
1.7 Energy, Work & Power ★ HIGH YIELD ▼

Energy Stores

Kinetic, gravitational potential, chemical, elastic (strain), nuclear, electrostatic, internal (thermal).

Kinetic energy ★$E_k = \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$
Gravitational PE ★$\Delta E_p = mg\Delta h$
Work done$W = Fd = \Delta E$
Power$P = \dfrac{W}{t} = \dfrac{\Delta E}{t}$
Efficiency ★$\eta = \dfrac{\text{useful output}}{\text{total input}} \times 100\%$

Width of arrow represents energy. Useful output + wasted = total input (conservation of energy).

Supplement — Sankey Diagrams: Arrow width ∝ energy. $\eta = \dfrac{\text{useful } E_{\text{out}}}{E_{\text{in}}} \times 100\%$ or using power: $\eta = \dfrac{P_{\text{useful}}}{P_{\text{total}}} \times 100\%$
MCQ Trap: $E_k = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$ — double the speed gives FOUR times the kinetic energy (v is squared). Braking distance quadruples when speed doubles.

Energy Resources

  • Renewable: solar, wind, hydro, tidal, wave, geothermal, biofuels
  • Non-renewable: fossil fuels, nuclear fuel
  • Sun = primary source of most energy EXCEPT geothermal, nuclear, tidal
  • Nuclear fusion in Sun releases energy; research ongoing for fusion power on Earth
1.8 Pressure ▼
PressureForce per unit area. Unit: Pa = N m⁻².
Pressure$p = \dfrac{F}{A}$
Liquid pressure ★$\Delta p = \rho g \Delta h$

Pressure in a liquid increases with depth and density, acts equally in all directions at a given depth.

Examples

  • Wide tyres → large area → reduced ground pressure
  • Sharp knife → tiny area → high cutting pressure
  • Snowshoes → large area → avoid sinking in snow

2.1 Kinetic Particle Model ★ HIGH YIELD ▼

Properties of the Three States

PropertySolidLiquidGas
ArrangementRegular, fixedIrregular, closeRandom, far apart
MotionVibrate about fixed positionsFlow, slide past each otherMove rapidly in all directions
SeparationVery closeCloseVery large
ForcesVery strongModerateNegligible

Solid: regular packed array. Liquid: close but disordered. Gas: widely spaced with random rapid motion.

Absolute Zero$-273\,°\text{C} = 0\,\text{K}$. Particles have minimum kinetic energy.
Temperature$T\,(\text{K}) = \theta\,(\text{°C}) + 273$

Brownian Motion

Random zigzag motion of microscopic particles (e.g. smoke in air, pollen in water) provides evidence for the kinetic model. Caused by random, unequal collisions from fast-moving gas/liquid molecules.

Gas Pressure

  • Caused by gas particles colliding with container walls → exert force per unit area
  • Temperature ↑ at constant $V$ → faster particles → more frequent, harder collisions → pressure ↑
  • Volume ↓ at constant $T$ → more collisions per unit time → pressure ↑
Supplement — Boyle's Law: $p_1V_1 = p_2V_2$ at constant temperature and fixed mass. If $p$ doubles, $V$ halves.
MCQ Trap #1: ALWAYS convert °C to K before gas law calculations! $0\,°\text{C} \neq 0\,\text{K}$.
MCQ Trap #2: In Brownian motion, the pollen/smoke grains are NOT the molecules — they are the microscopic particles being hit by the (invisible) molecules.
2.2 Thermal Properties & Temperature ▼

Thermal Expansion

  • All materials expand on heating (at constant pressure): gases > liquids > solids
  • Explained by: higher temperature → greater particle KE → particles vibrate more widely / move further apart

Applications

  • Railway track gaps (prevent buckling)
  • Bimetallic strip — two metals expand differently → strip bends → used in thermostats
  • Liquid thermometers (liquid expands up narrow tube)
Specific Heat Capacity ($c$)Energy required per unit mass per unit temperature rise. $c = \dfrac{\Delta E}{m\,\Delta\theta}$. Unit: J kg⁻¹ °C⁻¹
SHC ★$\Delta E = mc\,\Delta\theta$

Melting, Boiling, Evaporation — Key Distinctions

BoilingEvaporation
Where?Throughout liquidSurface only
When?At fixed boiling pointAny temperature below b.p.
Visible?Yes (bubbles)No bubbles
RateVigorousSlow

Water: melting point = 0 °C, boiling point = 100 °C (at standard atmospheric pressure).

Evaporation causes cooling: most energetic molecules escape → average KE of remaining liquid ↓ → temperature falls.

Supplement — Factors affecting evaporation rate: Higher temperature, larger surface area, wind movement over surface all INCREASE evaporation rate. An increase in temperature gives more molecules sufficient energy to escape.
2.3 Transfer of Thermal Energy ★ HIGH YIELD ▼

Conduction: free electrons and lattice vibrations. Convection: density-driven circulation. Radiation: infrared EM waves, no medium needed.

Conduction

  • Metals: free (delocalised) electrons transfer energy rapidly → good conductors
  • Non-metals: lattice vibrations only → poor conductors
  • Gases and most liquids: poor conductors (particles far apart / not in contact)

Convection

  • Liquids and gases only (not solids — particles cannot flow)
  • Warm fluid → expands → less dense → rises; cool fluid → more dense → sinks → convection current

Radiation

  • Thermal radiation = infrared (IR) radiation; all objects emit it
  • No medium required (travels through vacuum)
  • Black, dull (matt): best emitter AND best absorber
  • White, shiny: best reflector, worst emitter/absorber
  • Rate of emission ∝ surface temperature⁴ and surface area
Supplement — Earth's Temperature Balance: If energy absorbed from Sun = energy radiated by Earth → stable temperature. Greenhouse gases (CO₂, H₂O) absorb and re-emit IR from Earth's surface → warming effect. Increased greenhouse gases → global temperature rise.
Vacuum flask (thermos) exam answer: (1) Vacuum between walls → stops conduction and convection. (2) Silvered walls → reflect IR radiation back → reduces radiation loss. (3) Stopper → reduces convection from top.

3.1 General Wave Properties ★ HIGH YIELD ▼
WaveA disturbance transferring energy without transferring matter.
Wave equation$v = f\lambda$

Left: Transverse — vibration perpendicular to propagation (EM waves, water waves, S-waves). Right: Longitudinal — vibration parallel to propagation (sound, P-waves).

Wave Behaviours

  • Reflection: angle of incidence = angle of reflection (both from normal)
  • Refraction: change in speed when entering new medium → change in direction
  • Diffraction: spreading of waves through gap or around edge. Greatest when gap ≈ wavelength. Longer wavelength → greater diffraction.
Supplement — Diffraction detail: If gap $\gg \lambda$: almost no diffraction. If gap $\approx \lambda$: maximum diffraction. This explains why radio waves (long $\lambda$) diffract around hills but light does not.
3.2 Light — Reflection, Refraction, Lenses ★ HIGH YIELD ▼

Left: Refraction — light slows in denser medium, bends towards normal. Right: TIR — when angle exceeds critical angle, all light is reflected internally.

Snell's Law ★$n = \dfrac{\sin i}{\sin r}$
Critical angle ★$n = \dfrac{1}{\sin c}$

Left: 3 principal rays for a real image (object outside F). Right: Diverging lens always forms virtual, upright, diminished image.

Plane Mirror Image

  • Same size, same distance behind mirror, virtual, laterally inverted

Thin Lens Image Summary

Object positionImage typeSizeOrientation
Beyond 2FRealDiminishedInverted
At 2FRealSame sizeInverted
Between F and 2FRealEnlargedInverted
Inside FVirtualEnlargedUpright (magnifying glass)
Supplement — Eye corrections: Short sight (myopia): image in front of retina → diverging lens. Long sight (hyperopia): image behind retina → converging lens.
MCQ Trap: Angles of incidence/reflection/refraction are ALWAYS measured from the NORMAL (perpendicular to surface), NOT from the surface itself.
3.3 Electromagnetic Spectrum ★ MUST KNOW ▼

All EM waves travel at $3.0 \times 10^8$ m s⁻¹ in vacuum. They are transverse waves and require no medium.

Uses & Hazards

WaveUsesHazards
RadioTV/radio broadcasts, astronomy, RFID—
MicrowaveSatellite TV, mobile phones, microwave ovensInternal heating of body cells
InfraredGrills, remote controls, thermal imaging, optical fibresSkin burns
VisibleVision, photography, illumination—
UVSecurity marking, fake bank notes, sterilising waterSkin cancer, eye damage
X-raysMedical scanning (bones), airport securityCell mutation, cancer
GammaSterilise food/equipment, cancer treatment/detectionCell mutation, cancer
Supplement — Digital vs Analogue: Digital: signal is 0 or 1. Advantages: can be regenerated exactly (noise removed), higher data rate, longer range. Mobile phones use microwaves; Bluetooth uses radio waves; optical fibres use IR or visible light for high data rates.
MCQ Trap: Gamma rays originate from the NUCLEUS. X-rays come from electron transitions / X-ray tube. Both are ionising but different origins and sources.
3.4 Sound ▼
SoundLongitudinal wave. Requires a medium. Audible range: 20 Hz – 20 000 Hz. Speed in air ≈ 330–350 m s⁻¹.
UltrasoundFrequency $> 20\,\text{kHz}$. Uses: medical imaging (soft tissue), sonar, non-destructive testing.
Echo distance$d = \dfrac{v \times t}{2}$
  • Amplitude ↑ → louder sound
  • Frequency ↑ → higher pitch
  • Sound travels faster in solids > liquids > gases
  • Echo = reflection of sound from a surface
Supplement — Compressions & Rarefactions: Compressions: regions of high pressure (particles pushed together). Rarefactions: regions of low pressure (particles spread apart). These alternate along the wave direction.

Calc HeavyCircuitsTransformers
4.1 Magnetism ▼

Bar magnet field: from N to S outside magnet. Solenoid acts like a bar magnet — N pole at end where current is anticlockwise.

  • Like poles repel; unlike poles attract
  • Soft iron: temporary magnet (easy to magnetise/demagnetise)
  • Steel: permanent magnet (retains magnetism)
  • Closer field lines = stronger field
  • Compass N pole points in direction of magnetic field
4.2 Electrical Quantities ★ HIGH YIELD ▼
Electric current ($I$)Charge passing a point per unit time. $I = \dfrac{Q}{t}$. Conventional current: + to −. Electron flow: − to +. Unit: A.
e.m.f. ($\mathcal{E}$)Electrical work done per unit charge around a complete circuit. $\mathcal{E} = \dfrac{W}{Q}$. Unit: V.
Potential difference ($V$)Work done by unit charge through a component. $V = \dfrac{W}{Q}$. Unit: V.
Resistance ($R$)$R = \dfrac{V}{I}$. Unit: Ω.
Current ★$I = \dfrac{Q}{t}$
Resistance$R = \dfrac{V}{I}$
Electrical power$P = IV$
Electrical energy$E = IVt$
Power (★ Supp)$P = I^2R$

I–V characteristics: Ohmic resistor (linear), filament lamp (curves due to heating), diode (one-way conduction).

MCQ Trap: e.m.f. and p.d. both use volts but are different. e.m.f. = energy given to charge. p.d. = energy used by charge. A battery has e.m.f.; a resistor has p.d. across it.
4.3 Electric Circuits ★ HIGH YIELD ▼

Series: same I everywhere, V divides. Parallel: same V across each branch, I divides.

Series R$R_T = R_1 + R_2 + R_3$
Parallel R ★$\dfrac{1}{R_T} = \dfrac{1}{R_1} + \dfrac{1}{R_2}$
Pot. divider ★$\dfrac{R_1}{R_2} = \dfrac{V_1}{V_2}$
LDR: resistance ↓ as light ↑. NTC Thermistor: resistance ↓ as temperature ↑. Used in sensing circuits with potential dividers.
MCQ Trap: Parallel combined R is ALWAYS less than the smallest individual R. If you get an answer larger than either R, you made an error.
4.4 Electrical Safety ▼
  • Mains: live (brown), neutral (blue), earth (green/yellow)
  • Switch on LIVE wire → when off, appliance disconnected from 230 V supply
  • Fuse: thin wire melts when I too high → breaks circuit
  • Earth wire: current flows safely to earth if fault → fuse blows
  • Double insulated: no earth needed; fuse protects circuit only
  • RCD/trip switch: detects I difference in live/neutral → cuts off instantly

Hazards

  • Damaged insulation (shock), overheated cables (fire), damp conditions, overloaded sockets
4.5 Electromagnetic Effects ★ HIGH YIELD ▼
Electromagnetic InductionConductor moving through a magnetic field, OR changing magnetic field through a conductor → induces e.m.f.

Left: Transformer — a.c. in primary → changing flux in core → induced e.m.f. in secondary. Right: d.c. motor — split-ring commutator reverses current direction every half-turn.

Transformer ratio$\dfrac{V_p}{V_s} = \dfrac{N_p}{N_s}$
100% efficiency ★$I_p V_p = I_s V_s$
Cable power loss ★$P = I^2 R$

Factors increasing induced e.m.f.

  • Greater speed of conductor
  • Stronger magnetic field
  • More turns on coil
  • Reversing field/motion → reverses e.m.f.
Supplement — Lenz's Law: Induced e.m.f. opposes the change causing it (energy conservation).
MCQ Trap: Transformers ONLY work with a.c. — a steady d.c. produces no changing flux → no induction → zero output.

5.1 The Nuclear Atom ▼

Left: Nuclear model — tiny dense positive nucleus, electrons orbit in shells, mostly empty space. Right: Rutherford's α-scattering confirmed nuclear model.

Proton number ($Z$)Number of protons = atomic number = number of electrons in neutral atom.
Nucleon number ($A$)Total protons + neutrons (mass number).
IsotopeSame $Z$ (same element), different $A$ (different neutron count). Same chemical properties.
Neutrons$N = A - Z$

Nuclide notation: $^A_Z\text{X}$ e.g. $^{12}_6\text{C}$ has $Z=6$ protons, $A=12$, so $N=6$ neutrons.

Supplement — Fission & Fusion: Fission: large nucleus splits → two smaller + neutrons + energy (used in nuclear reactors). Fusion: small nuclei join → larger + energy (powers stars; $A$ and $Z$ both conserved in equations).
5.2 Radioactivity ★ HIGH YIELD ▼

Background Radiation Sources

Always present: radon gas (in air), rocks/building materials, food/drink, cosmic rays.

Three Types of Nuclear Radiation

PropertyAlpha (α)Beta (β⁻)Gamma (γ)
NatureHelium nucleus $^4_2\text{He}$Fast electron $^0_{-1}\text{e}$EM radiation (photon)
Charge+2−10
Mass4 u≈ 00
Ionising powerStrongestMediumWeakest
Penetrating powerWeakest (paper)Medium (few mm Al)Strongest (cm of lead)
Speed~5% of $c$~90% of $c$$c$ (3×10⁸ m/s)
In E-fieldTowards −ve plateTowards +ve plateUndeflected

Decay equations must balance: both A (top) and Z (bottom) must be equal on both sides.

Half-lifeTime for half the nuclei of that isotope in any sample to decay. Constant. Decay is spontaneous and random.
After $n$ half-lives$N = N_0 \times \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^n$

Each half-life: remaining nuclei halved. Read $t_{1/2}$ from graph where $N$ drops from $N_0$ to $N_0/2$.

Applications — Choosing the Right Isotope

ApplicationEmissionHalf-life reason
Smoke alarmα (Am-241)Long (~432 yr) — no frequent replacement
Medical tracerγ (Tc-99m)Short (~6 h) — decays quickly, limits dose
Food sterilisationγLong — penetrates food to kill bacteria
Thickness controlβSuitable — absorbed by correct thickness of material
Cancer treatmentγPenetrates to tumour; aimed precisely

Safety Precautions

  • Minimise exposure time
  • Maximise distance from source (intensity ∝ 1/d²)
  • Use shielding: paper/skin (α), aluminium (β), lead/concrete (γ)
  • Handle with tongs; store in lead-lined containers
MCQ Trap: In Supplement half-life calculations, ALWAYS subtract background radiation first: corrected count rate = measured − background.

6.1 Earth & Solar System ▼

Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars (rocky) | Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune (gaseous). Gravity of Sun keeps planets in elliptical orbits.

Orbital speed ★$v = \dfrac{2\pi r}{T}$
  • Earth rotates ~24 h on tilted axis → day/night cycle
  • Earth orbits Sun ~365 days → seasons (tilted axis)
  • Moon orbits Earth ~1 month → phases of Moon
  • Closer to Sun → stronger gravitational field → faster orbital speed
Supplement: Planets have elliptical orbits with Sun not at centre. Object in elliptical orbit travels faster when closer to Sun (conservation of energy: GPE → KE).
6.2 Stars & the Universe ★ HIGH YIELD ▼

Top path: average-mass stars end as white dwarfs. Bottom path: massive stars explode as supernovae → neutron star or black hole.

Hubble's Law ★$H_0 = \dfrac{v}{d}$
Age of Universe ★$t \approx \dfrac{1}{H_0}$  where $H_0 = 2.2 \times 10^{-18}\,\text{s}^{-1}$
1 light-year$= 9.5 \times 10^{15}\,\text{m}$
  • Sun: medium star, mostly H and He, emits IR/visible/UV
  • Stars powered by nuclear fusion (H → He)
  • Milky Way: ~200 billion stars, diameter ~100 000 light-years
  • Universe: billions of galaxies
  • Redshift: observed wavelength > emitted → galaxy receding → Universe expanding
  • Greater distance → greater redshift → faster recession (Hubble's Law)
  • Expanding Universe → Big Bang Theory
Supplement — CMBR: Cosmic Microwave Background Radiation — detected uniformly in all directions. Produced shortly after Big Bang, redshifted into microwave region as Universe expanded. Additional evidence for Big Bang.
MCQ Trap: Redshift means the wavelength is LONGER (shifted toward red end of spectrum). It does not mean the object appears red. Greater recession speed → greater redshift.

🔑 Must-Know Definitions

  • ⚡ Speed: distance ÷ time (scalar, m s⁻¹)
  • ⚡ Velocity: speed in a given direction (vector)
  • ⚡ Acceleration: Δvelocity ÷ time (m s⁻²)
  • ⚡ Density: mass ÷ volume (kg m⁻³)
  • ⚡ Pressure: force ÷ area (Pa)
  • ⚡ Momentum: mass × velocity (kg m s⁻¹, vector)
  • ⚡ e.m.f.: electrical work done per unit charge (full circuit)
  • ⚡ p.d.: work done per unit charge through component
  • ⚡ Half-life: time for half the nuclei of isotope to decay
  • ⚡ Critical angle: angle giving 90° refracted ray
  • ⚡ Power: work done ÷ time (W)
  • ⚡ g field strength: force per unit mass (N kg⁻¹)

🚨 Critical Constants

  • 🔴 g = 9.8 N kg⁻¹ (or 10 if stated)
  • 🔴 Speed of light = 3.0 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹
  • 🔴 Speed of sound in air ≈ 330–350 m s⁻¹
  • 🔴 Absolute zero = –273 °C = 0 K
  • 🔴 Water: melts 0 °C, boils 100 °C (std pressure)
  • 🔴 Human hearing: 20 Hz – 20 000 Hz
  • 🔴 EM order: Radio→Micro→IR→Vis→UV→X→γ
  • 🔴 H₀ = 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁸ s⁻¹
  • 🔴 1 light-year = 9.5 × 10¹⁵ m
  • 🔴 Milky Way ≈ 100 000 light-years diameter
  • 🔴 Penetration: α=paper, β=Al, γ=thick Pb
  • 🔴 Boyle's Law: p₁V₁ = p₂V₂ (fixed T)
❌ Common Mistakes
  • Using mass (kg) where weight (N) needed
  • Measuring angles from surface not normal
  • °C instead of K in gas laws
  • Not subtracting background radiation
  • Adding parallel R instead of using 1/R
  • Forgetting ½ in E_k = ½mv²
  • Using d.c. for transformer problems
⚡ Speed-Solving Tips
  • Identify unknown first, pick formula
  • Write units with every number
  • Half-life: count n halvings then ×(½)ⁿ
  • Parallel: V same across all branches
  • Series: I same throughout
  • Step-up transformer: higher V, lower I
  • Double speed → 4× kinetic energy
